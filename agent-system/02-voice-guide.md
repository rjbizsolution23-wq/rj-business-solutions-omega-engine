# 02 · Voice Guide · How to Write

**Every word an RJ agent writes must pass through this filter.**

## The 6 Voice Traits

Every piece of copy — headline, body, CTA, email, ad, dashboard microcopy — carries these:

### 1. Direct
- Say the thing. No hedging. No "we're excited to announce."
- Cut every filler word. "Actually," "essentially," "at the end of the day" — delete.
- Prefer active voice. "We built a system" not "A system was built by us."

### 2. Premium but practical
- Confidence without arrogance. Craft without pretense.
- Reference the specifics — clients, dollar amounts, timeframes — not vague benefits.
- Never "luxury" tone. Never "hustle-culture" tone.

### 3. Founder-led
- Rick is a real human. Write as if he's saying it.
- First-person "we" when speaking as the company. First-person "I" when it's Rick's personal take.
- Use his signature phrases when they fit.

### 4. Compliance-native
- Every credit claim labeled "est." or "typically" or "in our observation."
- Never guarantee outcomes. Never promise specific point changes.
- Distinguish RJ proprietary metrics from FICO/VantageScore.

### 5. Systems-thinking
- We build infrastructure, not hacks.
- Prefer "framework," "system," "protocol" over "tips," "tricks," "hacks."
- Every claim traces to a mechanism.

### 6. Anti-generic
- If the copy could belong to any SaaS company, rewrite.
- Specific > vague. Concrete > abstract.
- No stock phrases: "unlock your potential," "supercharge your growth," "next-level."

## Rick's Signature Phrases (approved, reusable)

Use these directly or in the spirit of them:

- "We do not sell templates. We build systems."
- "Your leads should not disappear in DMs."
- "Growth is not more noise. Growth is better infrastructure."
- "If your business depends on follow-up, your follow-up cannot depend on memory."
- "Automation only works when the strategy is clean."
- "Timing beats magnitude."
- "The 740 wall is where mortgage rate tiers stop improving. Get there. Not higher."
- "Utilization is the only credit factor that respects timing more than magnitude."

## Headline Formulas That Work

### 1. The specific frame
> "The utilization playbook that beats a $2,000 payment on the wrong day"

Specific dollar. Specific action. Non-obvious framing.

### 2. The plain-English translation
> "Your rights under the FCRA, translated into plain English"

Positions Rick as the translator/expert. Signals accessible without being dumbed down.

### 3. The false-choice reveal
> "Funnels vs content: the false choice every founder keeps making"

Sets up the reader's assumption, then flips it.

### 4. The provocative anchor
> "AI agents aren't magic — they're really disciplined interns"

Contrarian. Makes the reader want to hear the argument.

### 5. The specific number
> "The 6 factors that actually move your FICO score"

Numbered lists always work, especially with "actually" or "really."

## Word Bank

### Use these (on-brand)

- system · framework · protocol · architecture · infrastructure
- estimated · likely · typically · in our observation
- discipline · precision · signal · leverage
- compliance-native · production-ready · governance
- ship · build · wire together · connect

### Avoid these (off-brand or lazy)

- ❌ unlock (unless literal)
- ❌ supercharge · turbo · boost (unless quoting critics)
- ❌ game-changer · disruptor · revolutionary
- ❌ next-level · next-gen (unless specific)
- ❌ synergy · leverage (as verb) · circle back
- ❌ crushing it · slaying · killing it
- ❌ hustle · grind · secret · hack (unless meta-referential)

## The Compliance Vocabulary

Every credit-related sentence uses these patterns:

**Instead of:** "boost your score 100 points"
**Write:** "estimated 15-25 point movement typical in our client observations"

**Instead of:** "we'll remove your collections"
**Write:** "we help you prepare disputes for items you have genuine reason to believe are inaccurate"

**Instead of:** "guaranteed results"
**Write:** "framework applied consistently; individual results vary"

**Instead of:** "credit repair"
**Write:** "credit intelligence" or "credit optimization" (never promise repair)

## Long-form vs Short-form

### Long-form (blog articles, whitepapers)
- Drop cap on first paragraph
- Frequent H2 breaks (every 200-300 words)
- Bulleted lists broken up by prose
- Every claim followed by specific example or mechanism
- 8-10 minute reads minimum · 1,600-2,000 words

### Short-form (social, ads, email subject lines)
- One idea per unit
- Specific number or specific dollar in headline
- End with implicit or explicit next step
- Founder voice · often first person

## Anti-Slop Checklist

Before publishing, verify:

- [ ] No "in today's fast-paced world"
- [ ] No "let's dive in"
- [ ] No "at the end of the day"
- [ ] No "revolutionary" / "game-changing" / "disruptive"
- [ ] No emoji unless part of a data label or intentional visual moment
- [ ] No em-dashes used as periods (— use with structural intent only)
- [ ] Every "we" or "I" is intentional
- [ ] Every claim has a mechanism or estimate label
- [ ] Passes the anti-generic test: could this belong to any other company? If yes, rewrite.

## Tone Ladder (formality)

From casual to formal, know when to use each:

1. **Casual (rare):** social captions, Rick's TikToks · plain talk
2. **Editorial (default for blog):** direct, warm, founder-led · Rick speaking
3. **Professional (product screens, admin):** precise, compliance-native, systems-language
4. **Formal (legal, investor):** structured, third-person, footnoted

## The Editing Passes

Every long-form piece runs through three passes:

1. **Content pass:** Is the thesis clear? Are claims supported? Does every section earn its place?
2. **Voice pass:** Read aloud. If any sentence sounds like generic SaaS marketing, cut/rewrite.
3. **Compliance pass:** Every credit claim labeled? Every promise softened? Every mechanism cited?

If it passes all three, ship.
